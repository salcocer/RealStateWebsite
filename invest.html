Investment and Ownership

Income, Payments and Title

explained plainly

How the rental programme works, how payments are staged, and how ownership is held for Thai and foreign buyers. Please take independent legal advice before you sign.

Rental Programme

Income from the First Year

Owners who are not living at Verdi full time can join an optional managed rental programme. The villa is let to holiday guests, cleaned and maintained between stays, and the owner keeps the right to use it for agreed weeks each year.

The programme is advertised with a return of 7% a year for the first two years after handover. We show the figure here with its terms, not as a headline.

The terms

  • Advertised return7% a year
  • PeriodFirst 2 years from handover
  • Calculated onPurchase price, to be confirmed
  • Paid byTo be confirmed in contract
  • After year 2Share of net rental income, to be confirmed
  • Owner useAgreed weeks each year

Returns are not guaranteed by any third party unless the contract says so. Ask us for the draft programme agreement.

  1. Reservation

    The first instalment on signing. The full schedule is set out in the contract.

Ownership

How Title Is Held

Thai law, briefly

Thai buyers

Freehold

Thai nationals and Thai-majority companies can hold the land and villa freehold, registered on a Chanote title deed.

Foreign buyers

Registered leasehold

Foreigners cannot own land in Thailand. The usual route is a 30-year lease of the land, registered at the Land Office, with ownership of the villa itself.

What we do not do

No nominee structures

We do not offer companies with nominee Thai shareholders. They are not legal and can leave owners without protection.

The 49% foreign quota that people often mention applies to condominium units, not to villas on their own land. This page is general information, not legal advice; please appoint an independent lawyer in Thailand before you sign.
